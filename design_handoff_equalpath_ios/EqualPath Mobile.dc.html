EQUALPATH · iOSFORTY-ONE SCREENS · FOUR SECTIONS

The night before is
the whole product

Aina finds out on Wednesday evening that Thursday will not work. Everything the system does afterwards — options, confirmation, recovery — is downstream of that one moment. So the app is built around an evening, not around a calendar: it opens on tomorrow, and the week is somewhere you go, not where you land.

The reference illustrations gave the palette and the ornament — deep indigo, gold hairlines, cobalt and orange diamonds, four-point stars, a serif display voice. Ornament is spent on chrome only. Anything that reports a fact about a child's day keeps a plain sans face and a colour that means one thing.

COVERAGE IS A RING
A day is a closed loop, not a scroll. The gap is a wedge you can see at arm's length in a dark kitchen.
EVERY NUMBER NAMES ITS SOURCE
A handover gap prints the three inputs it came from. Unknown is a value, not a blank.
SEND IS A DOOR
Outbound messages live behind one screen with one button. Choosing a plan never sends anything.
FEASIBLE, NEVER BEST
Ranking is shown with its criteria attached, or not shown at all.
FIRST RUNTHREE SCREENS · LIQUID TRANSITIONS

Sign-up as one
continuous surface

The gooey reference does one thing well: touching shapes merge instead of stacking, and a moving element drags its own mass behind it. Applied here, the sign-up is a single blob that keeps splitting off the next step rather than three unrelated pages. The blob carries the brand; it never carries a fact.

MERGE, NOT STACK
Step dots fuse into one pill as they fill, so progress reads as one shape shortening — not four things counting.
MASS FOLLOWS THE FINGER
The primary button stretches toward the tap and settles back. It never lags long enough to hide its label.
ORNAMENT OFF AT THE DOOR
Liquid motion stops the moment a screen reports a child's day. Screens 01–09 stay still on purpose.

WHAT I CHANGED FROM THE SPEC'S SHAPE
The app opens on tomorrow rather than on a dashboard, because the promise is timeliness and a dashboard is a place you have to think to visit. The weekly pattern and the recovery flow — both required by the architecture, both previously unillustrated — now have screens. Plan comparison is a stack of three self-describing cards rather than a table, because on a phone at night a table is read as a wall.
OPEN QUESTIONS FOR YOU
Should Bahasa Melayu be a first-class toggle in the header, or set once in Me? Does the ring on screen 01 need a light theme for daytime use? And should screen 07 show a countdown to the reply deadline, or is a date enough — a countdown is more useful and more anxious at the same time.
